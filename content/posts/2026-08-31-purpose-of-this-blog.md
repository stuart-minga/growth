## The purpose of this blog

Over the next while, I want to use this site as a place to document my experience learning, growing and pushing myself to increase my developer skillset. I'm going to avoid writing these updates with AI (almost serving as a reprieve from its omnipresence), and try and speak to each step of this process - weekly at a minimum. I'll try and share wins, losses and learnings as I go! Additionally, I hope to write some thought pieces, also known as wild ramblings.

## My realization

It's become commonplace that 99.9% of the code put up for review in our workplace is AI-generated. That's not necessarily a bad thing, it's just _our world and we're living in it_. And when it comes to personal development, this has led to some clarity on what I need to be focusing on to keep growing as a developer.

It's a little easier nowadays to pump out features with the AI tooling that we have. The gap is in having the skillset to analyze what has been spewed out and recognize the contents for what they are. That's where I feel I can put in work to improve.

Particularly in the frontend space, there now lies a lot of value in being able to recognize flaws and enforce better standards by focusing on deep understanding of topics such as **performance**, **accessibility** and **security**, to name a few. Where in the past these items could perhaps become second class citizens and the focus could be _'just get the feature built'_, there is now a unique opportunity presented by our ability to slow down and think more as our robots do the grunt work!

Code review is our bottleneck, not code production, and that shouldn't necessarily be a bad thing. When we have time to slow down and think what we are building on a deeper level, I believe it can lead to better habits.

## What this means for me

I'd like to extend the thinking above into how I consider where I need to grow as a developer. My conclusion is simple - the onus is on me to gain wider proficiency as a whole. Yes, I may have a general idea of when something doesn't look or feel quite right, but I want to be able to think and present beyond that. I should be competent in expressing my knowledge coherently and become a respected and valued voice - one who can provide guidance and be a resource.

I want to not just blindly follow what LLMs tell me is best practice - I want to research, learn and form opinions which become core principles I believe in. To get to that point, I am aware I need to be willing to invest some time and energy into my development, outside of my regular day to day duties. Hopefully I and others I work with will reap the dividends of that investment.

## The end goal

The goal here is to elevate myself in my skillset and ability. It feels like the right time in my career to push toward more seniority, and as such I'm trying to put together a pathway which makes sense in a drive to obtain that. I have a sort of plan in place for the next year, and in my next post here I'll seek to outline it.
